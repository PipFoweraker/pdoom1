Key reasons

The redaction fixes HEAD and cannot fix what has already been distributed

The merged change is a straight file copy: 42 insertions, 42 deletions, 1,194 records before and after, identical id set, zero records carrying an email-shaped string afterwards. Every difference was a redaction already present upstream and absent here. That is a clean fix for anyone who clones or builds from now on. It does nothing for the addresses sitting in this repository's git history, in every existing clone, and inside v0.13.2 and earlier builds already downloaded. A distributed build cannot be rewritten at all. This is the same permanence property that keeps orphaned commit b68d6d3 alive in beacon-internal after a force-push.

The upstream fix existed for a day before anyone here could have received it

pdoom-data corrected this at source on 2026-08-02 in their #50. pdoom1's copy did not receive it, because godot/data/historical_events.json is a hand-commit dated 2025-12-25 and nothing syncs it. The script that ought to have carried the correction, scripts/sync_from_pdoom_data.sh, reads directories that no longer exist in pdoom-data and has never successfully run. So the corpus was frozen eight months ago, and the repair path was decorative.

The check that should have caught this class was silently disarmed

Worth knowing because it is not a pdoom1 defect but it explains why the addresses survived a scan. In pdoom-data's own account (coordination #10), a word-boundary escape written into a non-raw Python string through a shell heredoc compiled to a literal backspace character. The pattern could never match, so a PII residue check reported clean while ten records still carried addresses. It was invisible in terminal output because the character does not render, and it passed every ASCII gate because its codepoint is below 127.

Further reasons

The disclosure question is genuinely a judgement call and it is yours. The addresses were published by their owners on academic papers, so this is republication rather than leak, and the population is 42 researchers rather than a customer database. Against that: they did not consent to appearing in a game's data file, the material was distributed in a public binary, and the honest description of the current state is that it is out. My reading is that treating it as disclosed and moving on is defensible, and that pursuing notification would be disproportionate -- but I am not the right party to decide what is owed to people whose names are in the file.

On recommendation 3, the choice is between building a sync that actually runs and declaring the bundled corpus a deliberate snapshot that is updated by hand at known moments. The second is a legitimate answer and possibly the better one: the corpus is a one-time export supporting nineteen usable events, and a live sync would introduce a dependency for very little benefit. What is not acceptable is the present state, where a script exists, is documented, is referenced, and does nothing -- which reads as a working pipeline to anyone who does not open it.

Key reasons

Nineteen records out of 1,194 can open a window, and none of them is a headline

The corpus delivers 1,194 records; nineteen can produce a player decision, and 1,174 are demoted to an inert flavour feed whose effects are never applied. The nineteen are seventeen common and three rare. Zero are legendary -- all 41 legendary records sit in the arxiv stream that gets demoted to a grey line. The tier meaning "always fires, key story beat" is applied exclusively to records the game then whispers.

Both ranking fields are measurably inert

rarity is an exact text_length > 20000 threshold on a field that is not carried into the output, with correlation against the visible description length of r = -0.0065. significance, which scales every generated option's effects, scores 1,166 of 1,194 records at exactly 6. Two fields that look like ranking signals are, in practice, one constant and one coin that always lands the same way. Meanwhile pdoom-data already computes real salience tiers on its candidate feed -- A 172, B 687, C 1717, D 858 -- and does not put them on the collection we actually read.

One measurement contradicts three of our own documents, and it matters for ADR-0015

events.gd:290-310 intercepts a literal doom key and reroutes it into doom_system.add_stream_input("panic", ...), and its own comment names pdoom-data-derived events as the sole remaining producer of literal doom effects. WS3_FINISH_OR_DROP.md, TECH_DEBT_BURNDOWN.md and resource_accessor.gd all describe that path as an inert sink. They cannot both be right. This is load-bearing because pdoom-data's supplied vocabulary -- vibey_doom, stress, burnout_risk -- maps onto doom through our own variable_mapping.json, which is exactly what ADR-0015 outlaws, and it survives only because of that reroute.

Further reasons

Four fields are read by nothing. sources (present on all 1,194), tags, impacts[].condition (3,636 entries, every one null) and source_id are never accessed anywhere in the tree. pdoom_impact is copied onto the transformed event and then read by nothing, and 1,187 of its 1,194 values are null. If the answer to the contract question is "much less than you currently send", that is a good outcome and cheap for them to deliver.

Three variables are silently dropped and still count. ethics_risk (16 occurrences), technical_debt (12) and media_reputation (4) are unmapped, so their effects vanish -- but they still contribute to the significance sum that scales every option. A dropped variable that still moves the numbers is the same silent-wrongness shape as the rest of this week.

Three asks already on our record go unanswered, and none is a new request. ADR-0012, accepted 2026-07-12, names four event-content fields; pdoom-data supplies none, so everything defaults to deferrable. pdoom1 #630 records the feed drowning in arxiv entries and names salience tiers as the principled fix. TRIUMVIRATE_METABOLIC_CYCLE.md asks for monthly facts-only packs and states the split -- they own the fact schema, we own the shaping schema -- and pdoom-data has since ratified their side as their ADR-007 and ADR-008.

On recommendation 4, the trade is genuinely in our favour. They could encode our DQ-21 vocabulary directly, and their ADR-007 says they should not: if they encode frontier_capability and we later rename or split it, every exported pack already in the wild silently means the wrong thing -- no error, no failed parse, just numbers attached to a concept that no longer exists. An external taxonomy plus a mapping table on our side keeps our vocabulary ours to revise. That is the better deal and we should take it knowingly rather than by default.
