Key reasons

Your art verdicts were one disk failure from gone

tools/art_review/review_state.json was gitignored at line 137 as "local art-review app state". It is not local state: it is 2,713 curated verdicts and 111 notes, 1,687 of them added by you this morning, several of which read as briefs for the next generation round. Ignored, that work had no history and no second copy, and the merge script's .bak sits on the same disk. It is 465 KB of diffable JSON and it is now tracked -- which is also precisely the split ADR-0019 is built on, the index in git and the blobs outside. Git was carrying 5,267 art files and dropping the meaning attached to them.

The obvious fix to the event timing would make things worse

Under the current 52-turns-per-year assumption, everything dated 2022 or later never fires -- nine of the thirteen core promotion candidates, including four of the top five. The intuitive correction is to make the curve calendar-true at 260 turns per year, and that would render 95 per cent of the corpus unreachable. The workable fix is turns_per_year 26, offset 13, spread 6, which places the 2017-2025 arc across turns 14 to 222. Three numbers, and the wrong three are worse than none.

The intro asset you remembered as video is better than video

There are no video files anywhere in the repository. What exists is godot/assets/shaders/time_portal.gdshader: a procedural rotating vortex with fourteen tunable dials, three palettes, a tweened reveal, a live tuning harness and a deterministic capture tool. Its own header says it was built for the #801 cold-open. It ships in the pack today and is referenced only by two dev scripts. The recommended first intro build therefore costs zero assets and zero megabytes. In the same batch, fanfare_strategic_moves_v1/v3 were generated for the fanfare popup's image slot, marked keep, and never wired -- the slot exists, the art exists, and one to two hours closes your "just a black little screen" complaint from the 08-01 playtest.

Further reasons

Three "turns" share one word, and that is the unpicking you are dreading

clock.gd defines a turn as one workday, roughly 260 per year. doom_system.gd keeps its own 22 ticks per fiction month. rarity_curves.json assumed 52, which is weeks. Your own mental model is 12 per year, which is the ADR-0009 plan-month layer. All four are defensible and all four are called "turn" somewhere. The consequence, measured: your league run of 47 turns is about 2.1 fiction months, the best board score of 153 turns is about seven months, and the 2017-2032 arc you were pricing content against is roughly 3,900 turns -- some twenty-five times beyond the longest run anyone has achieved. I would not rewrite clock.gd; it is 187 lines, its constants are named and sourced, and #573 is a recent fix. The rot is in the consumers that never asked it. The cheap move is to forbid any module from naming its own turns-per-anything constant.

The historical corpus cannot carry the event volume, which confirms your instinct

At your own generous reading -- 180 month-turns with one or two interesting things each -- you need 180 to 360 event instances. The corpus realistically offers about 85. So history can be at most a quarter of the volume, and only if runs became far longer than they are. The bulk has to come from exactly what you listed: someone gets sick, a plane is delayed, a car accident, bureaucratic pain. Historical events are the anchoring texture, not the meal. That reframes the promotion pass as a flavour layer rather than the variety solution.

The updater design pre-empted two failures nobody asked it to look for

PR #1110, 1,072 to 1,089 tests, both guards proven to fail before being trusted. It moved the check off pdoom1.com/data/version.json -- a website file that must be synced from the game, the same cross-repo hop that rotted twice last week -- onto the release's own manifest, atomic with the assets. And it prefix-locked OS.shell_open to our GitHub URL, without which a tampered manifest could make the game open an arbitrary address on a player's machine. Integrity is sha256 per asset computed in CI, with nothing swapped without a hash match, and an honest limit recorded: #1069 means the hash proves what CI uploaded is what you got, not that CI packed the right bits.

I have changed how I generate file content

Coordination #10 rules that content be composed through file-writing tools rather than through a shell, and notes pdoom1 has had the same class of failure. The instance that persuades me is theirs: a word-boundary escape written into a non-raw Python string through a heredoc compiled to a literal backspace character, so a PII residue check reported clean while ten records still carried addresses -- invisible in terminal output, and below codepoint 127 so every ASCII gate passed it. I hit the same species four times today in mangled escapes, and routed around it only once. Adopted.
