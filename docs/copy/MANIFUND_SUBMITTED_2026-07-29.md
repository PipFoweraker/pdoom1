## 2. Published body (paraphrase + exact quotes -- see caveat above)

### Project summary

Described as "a nightmarishly hard, weird side project where players attempt
to battle an inexorable rise in...p(Doom)." Reflects challenges faced by AI
safety professionals, wrapped in gameplay that communicates urgency while
"mitigating the actual despair." Players cannot win but rather "buy time,"
with score determined by how long they delay failure. Draws on the creator's
game design interests, gaming background, governance project-management
expertise, and Global Catastrophic Risks operations career.

**New in the published version, not in the draft:** an alpha is live at
pdoom1.com with daily patches, transitioning to weekly updates as of
2026-07-31.

### Project goals

- Enhance reach through improved UX, UI, and infrastructure quality.
- Build community engagement among technically knowledgeable players who
  absorb AI safety concepts through gameplay.
- Support norm- and market-setting by "small and middle-power players"
  adopting positive actions.
- "watch people argue about how p(Doom) is calculated".

### Funding use

- Developer salary to lead gameplay elements.
- **Human artists to replace current AI-generated assets.**
- Assets and hosting costs.
- Community support infrastructure.
- Maintenance of the open historical AI-safety dataset underlying the game,
  available for researchers and other developers.

### Team and track record

Solo. Rugrats board game designed at age 8; played, judged and covered Magic:
the Gathering professionally, interviewing pro-level players; current
operations work in Global Catastrophic Risks with a governance
project-management background.

### Failure-mode risks

- Reputational damage could undermine AI safety discourse.
- May discourage better games from being created.
- Could inadvertently suggest capabilities are "maintainable" or that safety
  work can be deferred.
- Deliberately designed against through explicit disclaimers and strict
  factual accuracy in the dataset.

### Prior funding

"None. I have put in a fair amount of my own time over the last year and a few
grand of my own savings into it until now."

## 3. Decisions behind the fields (session record, 2026-07-29 1018-1038)

### Title and tagline

Pip's own steer (1030): the title field wanted a plain grant-style ask, not a
pitch line -- "something like 'fund development of pdoom1'" -- with the
descriptive tagline in the subtitle field. An earlier set of pitch-style
title/subtitle pairs was drafted against the wrong field structure and
discarded.

Tagline options offered, all built from Pip's own draft language and kept
under ~140 chars against truncation:

1. *A nightmarishly hard strategy game about holding back AI catastrophe --
   you can't win, you can only buy time.* (recommended)
2. *An AI-safety strategy game, and the open historical dataset it runs on.
   You can't win; you can only buy time.*
3. *You can't win, you can only buy time: a hard strategy game about the job
   real AI-safety people actually do.*
4. *Teaching AI safety the way Civ taught history -- a hard, weird strategy
   game where doom only ever gets delayed.*

Rationale for (1): leads with genre so a skimmer knows what they are funding,
and lands Pip's strongest line at the end where it sticks.

Deliberately kept OUT of the title: "Secret goal: watch people argue about how
p(Doom) is calculated" (a delight in the body, flippant in a title), and "the
subject matter isn't a costume" (strongest credibility line in the draft, but
it was `[glue]` text needing rewriting in Pip's voice).

### The ask

Pip's intuition (1034): "I don't really want to ask for less than 14k, and 48k
seems a fine max." Published as $14,500 / $48,000.

An earlier derivation had proposed a ~$5k floor. **Pip's higher floor is the
better call** and the reasoning is worth keeping: a minimum that is too low
invites a token grant carrying the same reporting overhead for a fraction of
the usable funding. 14k sits above the "why bother" threshold for both sides.

Line-item derivation, built so each figure is defensible if a funder asks
"why 14?":

| Line item | Min | Max |
|---|---|---|
| Hosting + infrastructure, 12 mo | $900 | $1,500 |
| Asset generation + tooling | $1,100 | $2,500 |
| Open dataset maintenance + update schedule, 12 mo | $3,000 | $4,000 |
| Pip's time (partial recovery) | $9,000 | $12,000 |
| Developer, part-time gameplay lead (~6 mo) | -- | $20,000 |
| Human artists, directed art pass | -- | $6,000 |
| Community support | -- | $2,000 |
| **Total** | **~$14,000** | **~$48,000** |

Scope story, one line each:

- **Min:** "I keep shipping monthly, the open dataset stays current and
  maintained, and I can justify a day a week on this for a year."
- **Max:** "Someone else leads gameplay for six months, real artists steer the
  look, and the dataset gets proper maintenance rather than my spare hours."

**Noted at the time, deliberately not corrected:** $9,000 for one day a week
across twelve months prices Pip's own labour at roughly $170/day, well under
what GCR operations plus governance project management commands. Conservative
by choice, not padded -- and useful evidence if a funder questions whether the
ask is realistic.

Budget was built natively in USD rather than converted from AUD, to avoid
introducing an FX error that would then need defending.
