# Orphaned verdicts in review_state.json -- what they are, and what they cost

**Sat 2026-08-15, seat `AlsoBort`** | diagnosis only. Nothing was modified, pruned or
deleted. `review_state.json` and `review_log.jsonl` were opened read-only.

---

## Summary

**The orphans are not deleted art, and no verdict has been lost.** Two separate
mechanisms produce them, and neither is attrition:

**1,400 `gen:` orphans are the `art_generated/` migration hold, seen for the first
time from the successor machine.** The art they name has never existed on this
laptop. It was never committed, never deleted, and never renamed -- it is the ~10,042
untracked files that `coordination/ESTATE_2026-08-12_repo-register.md` records as
existing only on Bort's disk. A clone-based migration brought the 534 tracked files
and nothing else. The register says the hold "is discharged when the untracked
remainder is on the successor machine and that has been checked there, not here."
**This report is that check, and the hold is not discharged.**

**1,863 `px:` orphans are the same file counted twice under two spellings.** Every one
of the 1,863 resolves to a file present on this disk right now. The browser gallery
writes `px:<dir>/<stem>.png`; `serve_review.py` writes `px:<dir>/<stem>`. Both keys
survive, the builder matches only the second, and the first is reported as an orphan.
Nothing is missing; the identity is doubled.

**The published 470 / 274 figures hold.** Both reproduce exactly from
`review_log.jsonl` for the documented session window. **One companion number does
not: "197 kept" is unreproducible, and the log says 195.** The art-cull campaign is
`approved: false` and unposted, so the correction is free if it is made now.

### Group B -- `px:` keys duplicated across two spellings (1,863 keys)

`art_source/` is fully tracked (5,267 files, all present), so no pixellab asset is

The correlation between spelling and writer is 1:1 across 6,329 keys, which is what

the cat in the alley.

## 3. Do the 470 / 274 figures still hold?

**Yes. Both reproduce exactly, and this is the part that matters.**

`docs/art/audit_2026-08-13/SESSION_2026-08-14_first-mass-review.md` defines the
session as 12:01:55 to 12:25:00. Recounting `review_log.jsonl` over precisely that
window, today:

| claim | published | recount | verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| events written | 496 | **496** | holds |
| distinct assets judged | 470 | **470** | holds |
| discarded | 274 | **274** | holds |
| kept | 197 | **195** | **does not reproduce** |
| 23 minutes | 23.1 | **23.6** active | holds, with a caveat |
| verdicts reversed mid-session | 12 | **12** | holds |

**The orphans do not touch these numbers, because the campaign counted from the
append-only log and not from the state file.** That was the right choice and it is
the reason a fifth of the state disagreeing with the disk costs nothing publicly. The
claim is about an event -- a person sat down and judged 470 things -- and the log
records that event with timestamps, prior values and notes. Whether the files are
currently on this particular laptop is irrelevant to whether the judging happened.

It is worth stating plainly how exposed that would have been the other way: **394 of
the 470 assets judged that night, 83%, are among the orphans.** A count taken from
`review_state.json` on this machine would have found 82 of them and reported a
session a fifth the size.

Three details behind the table:

**"197 kept" is the one number to fix.** Counting the final verdict per asset inside
the window gives 274 discard, 195 keep, 1 remix -- summing to 470, which is the
published total. Counting keep *events* instead gives 198, because three assets
reached keep and then moved off it. Neither route reaches 197, and the campaign's own
note reaches it via "472 assets carrying a final verdict", a total that also does not
reproduce. 195 and 198 are both defensible with their definition stated; 197 is not.
The pdoom1 session record independently says 198, so the two published sources
already disagree with each other.

**"23 minutes" is active time, not wall clock.** The session's first and last events
that day are 87.1 minutes apart, with five gaps over a minute and one of 49 minutes.
Sum the gaps capped at 60 seconds and you get 23.6 minutes, which is where the figure
comes from and why "one decision every 2.9 seconds" is honest. The copy says "judged
470 assets in 23 minutes", which a hostile reader could time-check against the log
and call wrong. "23 minutes at the keyboard" would be unimpeachable and costs three
words.

**Six more assets were judged later the same evening**, between 13:14 and 13:29,
outside the documented window. They are why a naive whole-day recount gives 476 / 275
/ 199 and why the numbers appear to drift. They do not invalidate the session figures;
they are a different, later sitting. The funding-breakdown draft already handles this
correctly by quoting the session as a closed event.

## 4. Is anything at risk of being lost?

**The verdicts are safe. The art is not, and 869 keeps currently cannot be acted on.**

`review_state.json` and `review_log.jsonl` are tracked, committed and pushed. Every
judgement in both groups survives on the remote. The 2026-08-04 decision to track the
state file is carrying its full weight here: had it still been gitignored, the orphan
half of it would exist on one disk that is being migrated away from.

What is exposed is the other side of the pair:

- **869 `keep` verdicts in Group A name art that is not on this machine.** 242 in
  `game_icons`, 144 in `an0807_l1_family`, 116 in `ui_icons`, and so on. A keep is an
  instruction to promote a file; with the file on another disk the instruction cannot
  be executed here. `godot/assets/icons/generated`, `.../images/heroes`,
  `.../portraits/generated` and `.../textures/generated` do not exist yet, so **no
  promote has ever run** and none of this value has been banked downstream.
- **439 notes in Group A** -- design direction in Pip's own words, including the
  founder-silhouette sentence the campaign quotes -- describe images that only exist
  on Bort. The note survives; the thing it is about may not.
- **Group B risks nothing.** Both keys, both verdicts and both notes are in the file;
  one is merely invisible to the gallery.

So the asymmetry is: lose Bort's disk and you keep every judgement and lose most of
what was judged. That is not nothing -- 274 discards are cheap to lose and 869 keeps
are not -- but it is much better than the reverse, and it is the outcome the tracking
decision bought.

## 5. Recommendation: leave the orphans in place. Do not prune.

Tidiness is the wrong frame, and this is the specific case where acting on it does
damage.

**The decisive argument is that presence-on-disk is a per-seat fact and
`review_state.json` is a shared file.** It is tracked, committed and pulled by every
seat. Prune it here, where the art is absent, and the next push deletes 1,400
verdicts from the machine where the art is present and where they are still live and
still actionable. **A per-seat observation must never drive a mutation of a
cross-seat file.** There is no version of the prune that is safe to run from
AlsoBort, and running it from Bort would find no orphans to remove.

The append-only argument runs the same way and is worth stating separately, because
it survives even if the migration completes. This repo's own art-cull copy makes the
case in public: the review tool appends rather than overwrites, twelve reversals were
caught on day one, and "under a plain state file every one of those earlier
judgements would have been gone". Pruning is that overwrite, applied to the store
rather than to a single key. A dead key costs bytes. A deleted judgement costs a
decision nobody can reconstruct, and 439 of the Group A orphans carry a note that
cost real attention to write at three seconds an asset.

The counter-argument deserves its hearing: **a state file that disagrees with the
disk by a fifth is a live source of wrong counts**, and this morning it produced one.
That is real. But the fix for a misleading count is not to delete the data -- it is
to stop deriving counts from the wrong file. The log is the record of what happened;
the state is a cache of latest-known-verdict. Every published figure should come from
the log, as the art-cull campaign already correctly does.

### Per group

**Group A (1,400 `gen:` keys) -- keep, and change what the builder says about them.**
The keys are correct; this checkout is incomplete. `build_full_gallery.py` should say
so: report orphans grouped by batch with a line naming the migration hold, rather than
a bare count and five example keys, which reads as attrition and was read that way
today. A batch that is 100% absent is a missing art root; a batch that is 3% absent is
attrition; the current message cannot distinguish them and the reader cannot either.
Cheap and mechanical: the grouping in section 1 is four lines of `collections.Counter`.

**Group B (1,863 `px:` keys) -- keep, and stop making more.** The repair already
exists in the codebase and is installed in the wrong place: `canonical_key()` in
`merge_gallery_export.py` reconciles the two spellings, but only on the merge path,
so every write through `serve_review.py` can still mint a second key for a file that
already has one. Move it to the write path (**A4**). Separately, the 373 disagreeing
pairs are a small vein of recorded mind-changes that no surface displays -- reconciling
them is a read-only report, not a mutation, and it should stay that way.

**Do not run any prune, on either group, from any seat, until A1 is answered.** Once
the art is on the successor machine, Group A stops being orphaned by itself, without
a single key being touched -- which is the clearest evidence available that the state
file was never the thing that was wrong.
