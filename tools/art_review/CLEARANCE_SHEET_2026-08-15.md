**1. `artq-028`** -- *"I have no idea what this is for and will watch out for
it"*

The one quote that needs no image, no context and no explanation, and the one
you already called your favourite in the LinkedIn draft. It is funny, it is
self-implicating rather than contemptuous, and it makes the reviewer sound
alert rather than dismissive -- the difference between "this is bad" and "I
will watch out for it" is the whole tone of the campaign. Safe on LinkedIn
because the joke is on the process, not on anyone.

**2. `artq-063`** -- *"This being on a lanyard is amazing. Strong positive
note. COnsider us using this in other employee_role icons. Consider
propogating this up into design documents. Amazing. A++"*

A post made only of discards reads as contempt for the work, and a funder
reads contempt as a risk. This is the counterweight and it is better than a
generic compliment, because it shows the mechanism: one accessory noticed in
three seconds becomes a rule for a whole icon family. Note the verdict was
**discard** -- the asset went, the idea stayed -- which is a more interesting
sentence than the one the devlog currently writes, and true. Fits Twitter at
172 characters.

**3. `artq-024`** -- *"Not nuclear symbol. Experiment with new, geometric
abstract represtation of doom escalation"*

The strongest funder-facing line in the corpus. It is a refusal of the obvious
symbol plus a specific direction, in eleven words, and it demonstrates exactly
the thing the money is for. Reads as taste with a reason rather than taste as
assertion. Nothing in it can be misread out of context.

**4. `artq-030`** (standing for `artq-031`, `-032`, `-033`) -- *"silhouette too
boviously male, too obviously signalling Operator"*

Not the best sentence, but the best *structure*: the same sentence typed four
separate times against four versions, and the third was kept anyway. That is a
designer hitting the same wall four times, which no single quote can show. It
needs its framing sentence, and I would never run it alone -- see never-publish
below. LinkedIn and devlog only, where there is room to say "four times".

**5. `artq-002`** -- *"this does not reflect what things do in reality and is
distressing to look at"*

The best short account of what is wrong with generated art, and it happens to
be about the physics of the thing rather than about style, which is the harder
and more credible complaint. One of only two in the ten with a confirmed
image: `art_generated/action_icons_missing/v1/grant_proposal_v1_1024.png`.

**6. `artq-025`** -- *"Not a lock. try a more symbolic reprsentation in the
abstract. Consider an alien culture's represetion of safe from boba
principles."*

Pairs with `artq-024` as the second half of a refusal-of-cliche beat, and goes
further: it asks for a symbol from a culture that does not exist. "from boba
principles" appears to be a mis-key and I have left it exactly as typed. If it
bothers you on LinkedIn, that is the row that most deserves a `text_light`,
and the only one in the ten I would say that about.

**7. `artq-064`** -- *"this si danerously close to a mtg symbol. think about
better repersentaiton for emotinal burnout"*

Three typos in one line, which is the pace argument making itself, and the
subject is humane -- burnout as a game state that deserves better than a
borrowed icon. The Magic reference is niche but legible to anyone who needs it
and skippable by everyone else.

**8. `artq-001`** -- *"poorl articulated design keeps leaning to weird
beiuoldings interpolating into each other destructively"*

The most vivid description of a generative failure mode anywhere in the set;
"interpolating into each other destructively" is the whole problem in four
words. The reason I have not put it first on LinkedIn is that it carries three
mangled words including one nearly unreadable one, and a funder meeting it as
the *opening* quote reads carelessness before they have been told about the
pace. Fine anywhere once a cleaner quote has gone first. Confirmed image.

**9. `artq-012`** -- *"i like the off-camera-ness of the pending weirdness"*

The best-written line in the corpus and the only one that sounds like a
sensibility rather than an instruction. It also does useful work: it shows the
review finding things it likes, in a vocabulary a model would not produce. No
image resolved, so it runs as text.

**10. `artq-054`** -- *"this will do for now, uninspired"*

Five words that concede something a promotional post normally hides: some of
what survived is not good, it is just not bad enough to kill yet. That
concession is what makes the other nine quotes believable, and it is cheap to
run because it cannot be misread.

**Reserve, #11: `artq-021`** -- *"at least 2 colour tones please"*. Weak on its
own, but this is the asset that went remix, remix, remix, remix, remix, then
keep. The quote plus the chain is the campaign's persistence story with a
voice attached. Promote it if you want that story quoted rather than described.

# Never publish

**No quote in the 67 names a person.** I checked all of them; the only named
person anywhere near this campaign is Austin Chen, who appears in the campaign
file, not in your notes. So the naming risk is nil and the list below is
entirely about tone and about claims that are not true.

- **`artq-030` to `artq-033` as a standalone quote, on any platform.**
  *"silhouette too boviously male, too obviously signalling Operator"* without
  its framing is a sentence about not wanting a man in the picture, and a
  hostile reader can stop there. With the framing -- four attempts, same
  problem, one kept -- it is a craft note about legibility of role. Publish the
  frame or do not publish the quote. Same applies to `artq-035` and `artq-036`
  ("andogynous operator please"), which are in the same family and weaker.

- **`artq-008` on LinkedIn, ever.** *"I hate this. Avoid avoid undo bad."* is
  the harshest string in the corpus. It is aimed at a sweep parameter and not
  at anybody's work, but nothing in the sentence says so, and it is the one
  line that would read as a person being cruel about art if it were quoted
  back at you. Devlog only, where the tail of the note explains itself. It is
  also dictated and outside the session -- see ask 4.

- **`artq-067` anywhere except the devlog.** The note asks for a version with
  "more religious overtones" and then "more state-propoganda overtines". In
  full, with its four lettered options, it is a designer exploring a space. As
  a pulled quote it is a headline about wanting propaganda aesthetics, and it
  is not worth the exposure for a note that is otherwise inside-baseball.

- **The LLM-delegation family: `artq-026`, `artq-027`, `artq-040`, `artq-041`,
  and the second half of `artq-014`.** These instruct a model to make design
  decisions -- *"an LLM should revisit the necessity for these assets"*,
  *"Entirely rebase instructions for this, reading LLM"*, *"revert to
  instructions and confirm back to user"*. The campaign's thesis is that taste
  is the part that does not come out of a model. Quoting these next to that
  thesis hands a critic the counter-quote, drawn from your own notes, in your
  own words. They are also the quotes where you sound like a system prompt
  rather than a person. Skip all five; the first clause of `artq-014` survives
  only if ask 3 is yes.

- **`artq-063` described as a keep.** Its verdict is `discard`. Publishing the
  quote is fine and recommended; publishing the sentence "one note is a keep"
  above it is a false claim about the log, checkable in one query by anyone.

- **`artq-005` to `artq-009` in any post that says the notes were typed at
  about 2.9 seconds each.** They were dictated at 13:29, after the session
  closed at 12:25, and each one carries "(Pip, dictated 2026-08-14)" inside
  its own verbatim text. Quoting them verbatim therefore *prints the
  contradiction* in the post itself.

# Four things found while preparing this

Each of these blocks or damages the drafted campaign and none is a matter of
taste.

**The devlog quote block was never re-copied.** The campaign's own
`notes-are-verbatim` guard records "Every quote re-copied from text_verbatim
after the ruling". All nine quotes in `devlog_long` are still the tidied
variants: "represtation" repaired to "representation", "mcuh" to "much",
"beiuoldings" to "buildings", "COnsider"/"propogating" to "Consider"/
"propagating", and `artq-025` additionally truncated to drop "from boba
principles". The LinkedIn and Bluesky blocks were fixed; this one was missed,
and the guard now asserts something false about it. This is the copy-becomes-a-
variant failure the file itself cites, inside the file that cites it.

**The devlog still says three silhouettes.** The correction of 2026-08-14 --
four versions, and the third was kept -- was applied to the LinkedIn copy and
not to `devlog_long`, which still reads "Three separate versions ... were
rejected" and omits the keep. Same root cause as above: one quote, two copies.

**`artq-014` is quoted as a fragment in two places.** Bluesky and LinkedIn both
run *"we ssem toh ave massively overindexed on the bankers lamp as an art
asset"*, which drops "this is getting better but " from the front and the
entire second sentence from the back, with no ellipsis at either end. It is
the better line for a post and I would keep it -- but it needs ask 3 answered,
because a verbatim rule with one silent exception is not a rule.

**The image-coverage note in the campaign is wrong, and wrong in the flattering
direction.** It says "only 34 of 67 quotes resolve to a versioned image
filename. 33 match by name only", which sums to 67 and implies every quote has
a picture. The actual counts are **4 versioned, 33 name-only, and 30 with no
image at all**. So the confirmed-image corpus is four quotes, not thirty-four,
and two of those four are in the ten above. Any visual sheet beyond those four
either needs `--allow-ambiguous` and your eye, or needs the art re-resolved.
