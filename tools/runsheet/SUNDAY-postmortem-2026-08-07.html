4. Mechanisms -- the through-line, tested

The brief proposed one mechanism: "a plausible output from a process that did not do the work." Tested against the eleven failures, it does not hold as a mechanism. It holds as a description of the outcome -- nothing complained. Naming the outcome is not naming the generator, and the difference is operational: there are four generators, with four different remedies, and no single remedy catches more than one of them. Plus a fifth item that is not a defect mechanism at all.

M1 -- The vacuous check

An instrument exists, is wired in, and the bad answer is unreachable. Instances: || true on the ladder heuristic; the grep whose | is a literal; the phase-guard regex; historically continue-on-error on every sync step and the CI gate that ran zero tests (#640).

Distinguishing property: removing the instrument entirely would leave the world no worse and the reader better informed. A bare claim invites scepticism; a claim with a green check attached buys credibility the check has not earned. Remedy: show it returning the other answer -- one control line, one red run.

M2 -- The absorbing empty state

A consumer reads a producer in the wrong shape, and the wrong shape maps cleanly onto a legitimate "nothing matched". Instances: --picks over 5,794 records; picks_target truncating 15; apply_review.py's 807 confident keeps of which 75 per cent could not move; the website's sync_icons() reporting success for about eight months while doing nothing.

Distinguishing property: no instrument is involved. There is nothing to harden -- the data path itself has a state indistinguishable from success. Remedy: zero is an error until proven; exit non-zero naming the store and the count read.

M3 -- The self-referential measurement

A number derived from a record of itself, then labelled as independently observed. Instances: cost constant to log to "MEASURED"; line numbers declared re-measured against a tree they were not re-measured against; "2,197" repeated from a sister repo without running a one-second command on a clone sitting on the same disk.

Distinguishing property: the number is real and traceable -- only its provenance claim is false, which is why it survives inspection. Remedy: derive from a committed generator, or carry an explicit provenance flag (cost_is_billed_truth: false).

M4 -- Escape mangling in a generated artefact

Invisible in the source, invisible in the output's appearance. Only a parser sees it. Instances: the gallery's \n\n; the \b that made a PII check report clean over ten records still carrying email addresses; the | eaten by basic grep (which is M1 and M4 at once).

Distinguishing property: looking harder does not help -- the 4 MB page rendered and every section drew. Remedy: parse the emitted artefact before writing it. Installed for the gallery; not yet for build_slot_picker.py.

M5 -- Destructive operation in a shared checkout

Not a plausible-output failure at all. Nothing looked fine; the work was simply gone and a person noticed. Listed because it cost real time, and because its existence is the cleanest disproof of the single-mechanism reading.

What the four share, and what they do not

They share an outcome and a discovery channel: every defect was found by a human or an agent going and looking, and none by an automated report. That is 07-31's one-sentence version, unchanged, which is itself a result.

They do not share a fix. M1 wants a control line, M2 a non-zero exit, M3 a generator, M4 a parser, M5 a worktree. A single remedy would have caught at most one of the eleven failures -- and a postmortem proposing one would be committing M1 in prose.

The prep is not in doubt: it demolished three of the agenda's four premises with measurements (the 1,072-event cost figure does not reproduce; nearest measured is 1,073, and the direction is inverted -- 975 unchanged, 101 arriving nine turns later, zero sooner) and found two live code defects on the way.

The room is much less clear, and the seat said so in advance: "pdoom1 is the repo Pip works in every day. Coordination should say so in the post-mortem rather than reading 3-0 as three independent derivations." A ballot among seats that had all read the same six public rulings of yours is not three independent votes. Verdict: keep the prep, question the room.

The claim-audit rule -- keep it, but its marginal value is narrow

Real catch, cheap: 6 wrong in 68, two in a merged PR body, one already in two other repos, one handed to another repo as an unblocking artefact. Against it, from its own section 5: the two artifacts that scored structurally -- a committed generator and a published reproduction formula -- both predate the rule. The honest reading is not "the rule fixed something" but "the repo already had the good pattern twice." Its marginal value is confined to places not already generated. And it produced M1 in its own body. Verdict: keep, with the two amendments -- re-run at merge, and show the command can return the other answer.

The phase-guard audit -- earned its cost on the hollow-guard catch alone

A near-null by design (0 confirmed reachable permalocks). It earned itself twice anyway: caught its own hollow regex before shipping, and found a live silent content-loss bug. It also declined to install a runtime check that false-fired in normal play -- "a guard that false-fires in normal play trains people to ignore it, which is worse than no guard." Verdict: the best-designed instrument of the cycle.

The taste profile -- the only instrument with a same-day cash payoff

Its signals are modest and it says so ("35 of 136 slots went the other way. This is a tendency, not a law"; median 1.7 seconds between decisions, so it may be a profile of what survives a fast glance). Its nulls are what paid: iterating a prompt bought nothing measurable (highest-vN chosen 40 times against an expectation of 47.3, p=0.34). That reshaped the art run the same day -- 264 images and USD 13.20 of re-rolls became 12 coherent directions at identical cost -- and killed the 08-03 "highest variant wins" convention. It also flagged its own money risk: the hue result does not survive Bonferroni correction. Verdict: the clearest earn in the pack.

Issue triage and mining -- the pair earned it; the triage alone would have done harm

The triage asserted six issues were absorbed by the UI architecture doc. The mining lane checked and found the doc contains zero occurrences of #622, #577, #936, #828, #830, #954, "monolith", "decompose" or "LET-DIE". Verdict on the pair: earned. Verdict on the outcome: nothing happened. Zero of the ~50 closeable issues were closed; the count went 201 to 208. An instrument whose output nobody actions is a cost with a report attached.

The guard-first practice -- cheapest instrument, highest hit rate

"Four lanes did this today; two of them found real bugs while doing it." Two of the three R1 instances were caught this way. Keep, and make it the default rather than a lane-level habit.
